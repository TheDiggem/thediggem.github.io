    <h1>Minneapolis Shooting: Protect the Children, Not the Politics</h1>
    
    <section>
        <h2>Intro: The Tragedy</h2>
        <p>On August 27, 2025, a gunman tore through Annunciation Catholic Church in Minneapolis during a quiet morning Mass. Two children—an 8-year-old and a 10-year-old—were gunned down, their lives stolen in seconds. Fourteen more kids and three elderly parishioners were wounded, blood staining pews meant for prayer.</p>
        <p>This wasn’t a war zone—it was a sanctuary, and two young lives will never see tomorrow.</p>
        <p>This isn’t about politics or pronouns. It’s about children who should still be alive.</p>
    </section>
    
    <section>
        <h2>Personal Stance</h2>
        <p>I’m 45, from a time when people kept their sexuality, politics, and religion close to the chest. I don’t care who you love, what you believe, or how you identify—live your life. That’s not my fight. My fight is for kids like the ones gunned down in Minneapolis.</p>
        <p>I’m pro-gun. I own firearms and stand by the right to bear arms. But I’m even more pro-kid: no child should die where they’re supposed to be safe—not in a school, a playground, or a church. Guns in the right hands protect; in the wrong hands, they destroy.</p>
        <p>Yet every time a tragedy like this strikes, the focus shifts. The media obsesses over the shooter’s identity. Politicians fuel culture wars. And while everyone screams, the truth gets buried: two kids are dead, and their names should matter more than any headline.</p>
    </section>
    
    <section>
        <h2>The Problem</h2>
        <p>Guns are part of America. Millions of people own them responsibly—hunters, sport shooters, parents protecting their homes. Criminals don’t follow laws, so bans won’t erase guns from their hands. That’s one reality.</p>
        <p>Here’s another: firearms are the leading cause of death for U.S. kids and teens<a href="https://www.cdc.gov/firearm-violence/data-research/facts-stats/index.html" class="citation" target="_blank">[1]</a> (CDC, 2022—recent data shows the trend persists<a href="https://publichealth.jhu.edu/2024/guns-remain-leading-cause-of-death-for-children-and-teens" class="citation" target="_blank">[2]</a>). Over 2,500 children and teens (ages 1-17) die from gunshots each year<a href="https://publichealth.jhu.edu/center-for-gun-violence-solutions/annual-gun-violence-data-2022" class="citation" target="_blank">[3]</a>—roughly 60% from homicides, 25% from suicides, and the rest from accidents or undetermined causes. The Minneapolis shooting, where two kids were murdered in a church, is a brutal reminder of that toll.</p>
        <p>We’re losing kids not just to bullets but to broken systems. Mental health care is underfunded and overstretched. Families are cracking under pressure. Politicians bicker while caskets are built for children.</p>
        <p>If someone’s so lost they’d walk into a church and kill kids, the failure started long before the first trigger pull. We need red flag laws, better screening, and communities willing to step in when they see the warning signs—not to strip rights, but to stop monsters from making their pain everyone else’s tragedy.</p>
    </section>
    
    <section>
        <h2>The Solution</h2>
        <p>So what do we do?</p>
        <ul>
            <li>Stop hijacking every tragedy for politics. Two kids are dead. Their lives are worth more than another fight on cable news.</li>
            <li>Strengthen mental health resources. Give people help before they turn their anger outward.</li>
            <li>Keep guns out of the wrong hands without punishing the right ones. Smarter background checks. Safer storage. Penalties for leaving guns unsecured.</li>
            <li>Put children first. If schools, churches, and playgrounds aren’t safe, then nothing else we argue about matters.</li>
        </ul>
    </section>
    
    <section>
        <h2>Closing</h2>
        <p>Maybe this makes some people uncomfortable. Maybe it pisses some off. Good. Because two kids lost their futures, and others are scarred for life.</p>
        <p>My view is simple: no child should die in a place of worship. No child should be caught in adult violence. No child should be sacrificed to the noise of politics.</p>
        <p>Until we put children above culture wars, we’re failing our kids. Let’s make their safety our fight.</p>
    </section>
